# Jobs-To-Be-Done (JTBD) & Personas

## Persona A: Self-directed retail investor
* **Situation**: Managing their own portfolio, making occasional trades based on news or gut feeling.
* **Job**: "Help me realize when I'm making emotional decisions so I don't lose money on bad trades."
* **Pain**: Constantly buying high and selling low, feeling regret after trades.
* **Desired outcome**: Better returns, peace of mind, feeling in control of their financial destiny.
* **Current workaround**: Post-trade regret, unstructured journaling, or doing nothing.
* **Why tools fail**: Most tools just show P&L and charts, not *why* the P&L is what it is.
* **Where BiasGuard helps**: Highlighting emotional trading patterns (e.g., holding losers too long).
* **Where it doesn't**: Won't tell them *what* to buy or sell.

## Persona B: Quant/analytical investor
* **Situation**: Heavily relies on data, screens, and technical indicators.
* **Job**: "Provide meta-analytics on my trading strategy to optimize my edge."
* **Pain**: Cannot easily track their own adherence to their systematic rules (discipline).
* **Desired outcome**: A higher Sharpe ratio, elimination of unforced errors, statistical proof of discipline.
* **Current workaround**: Complex Excel spreadsheets tracking expected vs. actual execution.
* **Why tools fail**: Spreadsheets require heavy maintenance; standard brokerages don't measure discipline.
* **Where BiasGuard helps**: Providing statistical evidence of behavioral drift.
* **Where it doesn't**: Won't replace their backtesting software or advanced charting.

## Persona C: Behavior-conscious investor
* **Situation**: Aware of cognitive biases (has read Kahneman) but struggles to apply it to their own money.
* **Job**: "Act as a mirror for my financial decisions to keep my biases in check."
* **Pain**: Knowing they are biased but lacking the real-time feedback to stop it.
* **Desired outcome**: Alignment between their rational intentions and actual actions.
* **Current workaround**: Reading behavioral finance books, discussing with peers.
* **Why tools fail**: Books are theoretical; they don't analyze personal brokerage data.
* **Where BiasGuard helps**: Bridging the gap between theory (knowing the bias) and practice (seeing it in their data).
* **Where it doesn't**: Won't act as a certified financial planner or therapist.

---

## Anti-Personas
* **Day Traders**: Need real-time, millisecond execution and tick-by-tick charting. BiasGuard is not a high-frequency trading dashboard.
* **Tax Users**: Looking solely for tax-loss harvesting or CPA reports. BiasGuard is for behavior, not tax filing.
* **Advice Seekers**: Looking for "buy this, sell that" stock tips or robo-advising. BiasGuard analyzes past behavior, it does not provide forward-looking investment advice.
